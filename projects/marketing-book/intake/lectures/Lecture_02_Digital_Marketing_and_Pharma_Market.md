## Digital Marketing

**Digital marketing**, also called online marketing, is "the promotion of brands to connect with potential customers using the internet and other forms of digital communication."

### Digital Channels

Online channels such as search engines, social media, email, applications, websites, and any new digital channels that may arise with the possibility of advertising.

### Benefits of Digital Marketing

- Global online reach and visibility
- Effective targeting
- Measurable
- Cost
- Variety in digital marketing strategies
- Increased engagement
- Speed
- Analytics and optimization
- Easy to start

### Objectives of Digital Marketing

1. Build brand value and awareness
2. Improve search engine optimization (SEO) techniques
3. Increase the number of visitors
4. Paid advertisement
5. Produce unique and relevant insights regularly
6. Reduce acquisition costs for new customers

### Digital vs. Traditional Marketing

| Digital Marketing | Traditional Marketing |
|---|---|
| Cost-effective | Expensive |
| A large audience | Limited audience |
| Variety of sources | Non-versatile |
| Higher conversion rate | Low conversion rate |
| Measurable results | Results cannot be measured |
| Immediate communication | Delayed communication |

## Characteristics, Structure and Organization of the Pharmaceutical Market

**Pharmaceutical marketing:** the marketing of drugs or products to medical practitioners and consumers alike.

Traditionally, Rx and OTC marketing has taken various forms: free samples and sales reps, fliers and literature, events, continuing medical education, etc.

---

## Consumer Triggers for Buying Medicines

### 1. Medical Advice

For Rx drugs, prescriptions still drive the highest number of sales. The questions for pharmaceutical marketers are:

- How do you drive a higher number of prescriptions?
- How do you make sure the consumer follows the prescription and keeps your brand in mind next time?

### 2. Heuristic Triggers (family and friends)

Familiar brands or medicines that have always been used or kept at home, friends' recommendations of medicines that worked for them, or one's own previous experience often drive purchases, rather than a rational evaluation.

### 3. Symptomatic Triggers

Consumers are more aware than ever of their symptoms and the medicines available. Many research online, then go to pharmacies and ask for OTC solutions to their problems.

### 4. Advertising

Information provided by the company, written information on the product's leaflet, product pricing, etc., that creates a preference for one product over another.

## Comparison Between Pharmaceutical and Consumer Markets

| Criterion | Pharmaceutical Markets | Consumer Markets |
|---|---|---|
| Is the consumer the decision maker? | ✗ No | ✓ Yes |
| Does the consumer pay directly for the product? | ✗ No | ✓ Yes |
| Brand loyalty | Higher | Lower |
| Importance of ethics | ✓ Yes (high) | ✗ Lower |
| Degree of government regulation | Higher | Lower |
| Research & development (complexity, human necessity and costs) | High | Lower |
| Price sensitivity | Lower | Higher |

Also shown: the seller–buyer relationship (consideration exchanged between seller and buyer).

**Price sensitivity** = % change in quantity ÷ % change in price

## Principles of Marketing Management of the Pharmaceutical Enterprise

1. Creation, production and distribution of effective medical products.
2. Elimination or minimizing of medicines' side effects.
3. Use of high-quality raw materials in manufacturing medical products.
4. Development of new medicines according to consumers' needs (e.g., generic vs. brand).
5. Development of an optimum pricing policy for medical products.
6. Prevention and treatment of the population's diseases — and, finally, the nation's health, which is one of the prime interests of a society.

## Problems of Pharmaceutical Marketing

1. Complex study of the market.
2. Revealing demand and unsatisfied needs.
3. Planning the commodity assortment and prices (supply and demand equilibrium).
4. Planning and carrying out selling (distribution from factory through stores to consumers).
5. Manufacturing organization.
