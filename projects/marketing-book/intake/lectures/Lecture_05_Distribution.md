## Pharmaceutical Sales Companies

### Pharmacy Stores (Warehouses / "Databases")

A health-system establishment operating under a licence for the retail and wholesale distribution of medicinal products, medical products and other pharmaceutical products, supplying pharmacies, medical institutions and other organizations with drugs and medical products.

**Functions of pharmacy stores:**

1. Obtaining medical and pharmaceutical products from manufacturers and legal entities of different forms of ownership.
2. Carrying out export–import and goods exchange.
3. Ensuring quality control of medicines.
4. Providing proper storage conditions based on the products' physicochemical properties.
5. Supplying medicines to pharmacies and health-care institutions.
6. Providing transportation of goods.

## Retail Trade

- Pharmacies are the retail outlets for medicines.
- **Retail trade:** any business activity related to selling goods and services to final consumers for personal, non-commercial use.

### Functions of Retail Trade

- Studying prevailing market conditions
- Determining supply and demand for specific types of goods
- Searching for products needed for retail trade
- Sorting goods to prepare the assortment for sale
- Paying suppliers for goods received
- Receiving, storing, labelling and pricing products

### Marketing Decisions in Retail Trade

- Selecting the target market
- Developing product lines
- Providing a range of services and a pleasant shopping atmosphere
- Setting retail prices
- Advertising and information

## Distribution

- **Distribution:** the company's activity of planning, organizing and controlling the physical movement of materials and finished products from their place of manufacture to the point of use, to meet consumer needs and earn profit.
- **Distribution channel:** a set of firms or individuals who take on, or help transfer, ownership of a product or service on its way from producer to consumer. Channel participants are also called **intermediaries**.

### Tasks of Distribution Policy

| Strategic tasks | Tactical tasks |
|---|---|
| 1. Planning (forecasting) future distribution channels | 1. Working with existing customers and attracting new ones |
| 2. Selecting systems and methods of marketing and related types of channels | 2. Searching for and selecting commercial offers for the supply of goods |
| 3. Routing of sales | 3. Organizing orders, including transportation and freight/cargo-handling operations |
| 4. Establishing a network of wholesale and retail outlets | 4. Ensuring the effectiveness of marketing |

### Functions of Marketing Channels

1. **Reporting:** collecting and processing information from market research for planning and organizing distribution.
2. **Promotion:** developing and spreading information about products through marketing communication to attract buyers.
3. **Contacts and negotiation:** building relationships with potential buyers; agreeing on prices, volumes and delivery terms.
4. **Ordering:** concluding contracts with other channel members to purchase products from the manufacturer, and monitoring them.
5. **Adapting products to customer requirements:** production, sorting, packing and wrapping, assembling, after-sales service.
6. **Physical movement of goods:** organizing transportation and warehousing.
7. **Financing:** finding and allocating the funds needed to cover the channel's operating costs.

### Choosing a Distribution Channel

- The choice depends on **costs and profits**.
- With external channels, both costs and benefits are shared among all channel participants.

## Classification of Distribution Channels for Pharmaceutical Products

| Level | Channel |
|---|---|
| **Zero-level** | Producer (pharmaceutical companies, firms, factories) → Consumer (health-care facilities, inpatients and outpatients, other people) |
| **One-level** | Producer → Pharmacy → Consumer<br>Producer → Hospital pharmacy → Consumer (health-care facilities, inpatients) |
| **Two-level** | Producer → National-level wholesale intermediary → Pharmacy → Consumer (outpatients, other people) |
| **Three-level** | Producer → National-level wholesale intermediary (the importer) → Regional-level wholesale intermediary → Pharmacy → Consumer (outpatients, other people) |

## Types of Intermediaries in the Pharmaceutical Market

```
Intermediaries (wholesale enterprises)
├── Wholesale intermediaries
│   ├── Independent (own the goods): Distributors, Dealers
│   └── Dependent (do not own the goods): Brokers, Commissionaires,
│       Consignees, Wholesale (trade) agents
└── Retail intermediaries: Pharmacies and their subdivisions
```

### Independent Intermediaries (with ownership of goods)

**Wholesaler**

- A legal or natural person who receives large quantities of goods from different manufacturers and organizes their movement to retail or directly to consumers.
- Usually the owner of the goods; profit comes from the difference between buying and selling price.

**Distributor**

- A company or person mainly performing import operations, acting as a dealer under contracts with producers (sellers) and buyers.
- Buys and sells in its own name and at its own expense; usually located in the importing country.

**Dealer**

- An independent intermediary specializing in durable goods that need considerable after-sales service; operates in its own name and at its own expense.

**Retailer**

- A legal or natural person who sells goods directly to the final consumer, having acquired them from a wholesaler or manufacturer.

### Dependent Intermediaries (no ownership of goods sold)

**Broker**

- Takes part in the sale by bringing buyer and seller together; receives a commission (brokerage) from the completed deal.
- Does not buy goods in their own name and takes no responsibility for them.

**Commissionaire**

- Has a warehouse and, for a fee, sells goods for the benefit and at the expense of the owner, but in their own name.
- Paid as a percentage of the transaction, or the difference between the principal's set price and the sale price.

**Consignee**

- Differs from a commissionaire: takes the owner's goods into their warehouse and sells them in the owner's name but at their own expense.
- Paid as goods are sold from the warehouse, depending on the type of consignment (simple, partially used, returned).

**Wholesale agent**

- Works with the manufacturer under a specific agreement, operating at its own expense and on its own behalf; may be granted exclusive selling rights in some regions.
- Remuneration is set by the principal, usually a percentage of the deal.
