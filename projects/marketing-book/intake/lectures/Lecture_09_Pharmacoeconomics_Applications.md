## Example 1 — Standardization Cost Value

| Medical resource | Units of each resource | Cost per unit |
|---|---|---|
| Doctor visits | 8 visits | $20 per 2 visits |
| Medications | 28 capsules | $1.05 per 4 capsules |
| Hospitalization | 7 nights | $50 per night |

**Question:** What is the total standardization cost value?

### Answer

- **Doctor visits:** 8 ÷ 2 = 4 units → 4 × 20 = **$80**
- **Medication:** 28 ÷ 4 = 7 units → 7 × 1.05 = **$7.35**
- **Hospitalization:** 7 × 50 = **$350**

**Total standardization cost value = 80 + 7.35 + 350 = $437.35**

## Example 2 — Choosing CMA vs. CEA

| Medication | Cidophage | Glucophage | Glimepiride |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dosage form | Tablet | Tablet | Tablet |
| Strength | 1 g | 1 g | 1 mg |
| Cost (LE) | 18 | 35 | 27 |
| Effectiveness | Lowers fasting blood glucose by an average of 25% | Lowers fasting blood glucose by an average of 25% | Lowers fasting blood glucose by an average of 15% |

**Questions:**

1. Which analysis can be used to compare medications 1 & 2? Why?
2. Which analysis is suitable to compare medications 1 & 3?
3. Apply this method to them.

### Answer

1. **CMA** — the two medications are equivalent (same outcome), so we simply compare cost.
2. **CEA** — the two medications have the same type of outcome (lowering blood glucose) but different effectiveness.
3. CER = Difference in costs (1 & 3) / Difference in benefits (1 & 3) = (18 − 27) / (25 − 15)

   *The slide gives the result as −15.36; the arithmetic gives **−9 / 10 = −0.9 LE per 1% reduction**. Either way, the result is negative.*

**Interpretation:** a **negative** incremental CER means one treatment — the **dominant** option — is both **more effective and less expensive** than the other (the **dominated** option). Here, Cidophage dominates Glimepiride.

## Example 3 — Cost-Benefit Analysis

| Type of cost | Treatment A | Treatment B |
|---|---|---|
| Doctor visits | $10 | $15 |
| Cost of IV supplies | $5 | $7 |
| Cost of drug | $30 | $20 |
| Cost of transportation | $15 | $40 |
| **Total benefits** | **$30** | **$35** |

**Question:** Calculate the CBA for treatments A & B.

### Answer

- Total cost of treatment A = 10 + 5 + 30 + 15 = **$60**
- Total cost of treatment B = 15 + 7 + 20 + 40 = **$82**
- Benefit (B − A) / Cost (B − A) = (35 − 30) / (82 − 60) = 5 / 22 = **0.23**

**Decision rule:**

- B/C ratio **> 1** → the intervention is beneficial.
- B/C ratio **≤ 1** → not beneficial.

Here, 0.23 < 1, so the extra cost of treatment B over A is **not beneficial**.
