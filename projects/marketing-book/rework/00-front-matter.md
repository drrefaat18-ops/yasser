# Healthcare Marketing

**Marketing and Health Economics for Applied Health Sciences** · 2026–2027

Assoc. Prof. Mohamed Yasser Hamdy

Faculty of Applied Health Sciences, East Portsaid National University

This book is for teaching. Prices, regulations and reimbursement rules change; check the current official sources before applying any figure in practice.

## About This Book

This book is written for third-year students of the Faculty of Applied Health Sciences. Most of you will work in medical laboratories and imaging centres, or in other health services. You know your service from the bench and the scanner. This book shows you the same service from three other sides: the patient who chooses it, the organisation that runs it, and the payer who funds it.

Marketing in health care is not the same as selling. It starts from what patients and doctors need, and it works within strict ethical and legal limits. A laboratory that gives accurate results, short waits and clear reports is marketing well, even if it never advertises. A laboratory that pushes unneeded tests is marketing badly, however good its advertisements.

The book has three parts. Part I explains what marketing is and why the healthcare market differs from other markets, and it covers digital marketing. Part II takes the marketing mix apart for health services: product, life cycle, price, place and promotion. It uses the seven Ps of services marketing, because people, process and physical evidence are what patients actually meet. Part III turns to health economics: how to count costs, how to measure outcomes, and how to judge whether a medicine, test or scan is worth what it costs.

Every chapter applies its ideas to health services, not only to medicines. Examples come from laboratories, imaging centres, hospitals and pharmacies, and prices are in Egyptian pounds unless stated otherwise. All prices and effects in worked examples are illustrative, not real market data.

## How to Use This Book

Each chapter opens with learning objectives. Read them first; they tell you what you should be able to do by the end.

Three boxes appear in every chapter. **Through Two Lenses** applies the chapter's main idea to the medical laboratory and to radiology and medical imaging. *In Practice* shows the idea at work in a health service. *Ethics Check* sets out where the idea meets a professional or legal limit. Read the Ethics Check with particular care: it is the part of marketing that most distinguishes health care.

Other boxes appear where they help. *Key Formula* gives an equation with its symbols. *Worked Example* works a calculation step by step. *Common Mistake* names an error that students often make. *Egyptian Context* links the topic to the Egyptian health system.

Each chapter ends with key takeaways, ten multiple-choice questions and three essay questions. Answer the questions before reading the answers. Every multiple-choice answer has a short rationale, and every essay question has model answer points. Use the points to check your own answer, not to memorise.

Work the calculations in Part III with a pen. The steps are shown in full, so if your answer differs you can find the line where it went wrong.

Terms printed in bold are defined in the glossary at the end of the book. The consolidated reference list follows the glossary.
