| C-001 | major | Chapter 7, §§7.3 and 7.7; Q1 and its key | The book classifies Egyptian medicine prices as “controlled prices” subject only to a ceiling. Egypt instead applies compulsory official pricing: the authority determines each product’s public price, which pharmacies may not freely alter. This fits the book’s own definition of a fixed price, not its ceiling-only definition of a controlled price. Official descriptions confirm authority-determined pricing ([EDA](https://edaegypt.gov.eg/en/media-center/news/chairman-of-the-drug-authority-the-egyptian-state-supports-the-provision-of-all-pharmaceutical-needs-for-the-egyptian-patient-and-the-egyptian-pharmaceutical-products-with-the-highest-quality-standards/), [Ministry of Health](https://www.mohp.gov.eg/NewsDetails.aspx?subject_id=2547)). | It teaches the wrong Egyptian pricing regime and makes Q1 wrong under the textbook’s own categories. | Describe registered medicines as compulsorily/fixed priced at the officially approved public price, or redefine the categories explicitly. Rekey or rewrite Q1. |
| C-002 | major | Chapter 6, §6.8 Egyptian Context; Q10 and its rationale | The text extends the cited EDA *Prescription Medicine Promotion Guidelines* to “medicines, devices and in-vitro diagnostic products,” and Q10 makes the rule apply to any “new product.” The cited guideline’s scope is registered human prescription-only medicines and HCP-directed promotional material—not devices or IVDs ([EDA guideline](https://www.edaegypt.gov.eg/media/jijbc3lf/edrex-gl-cap-care-003-prescription-medicine-promotion-guidelines-2020_1.pdf)). | This is an unsupported regulatory generalisation embedded in an assessed item. Students could wrongly apply a prescription-medicine rule to other regulated product categories. | Narrow the passage and Q10 to prescription medicines, or cite and explain the separate device/IVD provisions that establish an equivalent rule. |
| C-003 | minor | Chapter 4, §§4.1–4.2, versus Chapter 7, §7.1 | Chapter 4 defines the Price element as money plus time and effort, while Chapter 7 defines price as the monetary amount and identifies time, travel and effort as broader customer costs. | The same core term changes meaning between chapters, encouraging students to confuse monetary price with total customer cost. | Define the Price P consistently as the monetary charge; discuss time, travel and effort separately as non-monetary customer costs. |
| C-004 | minor | Chapter 11, E3 model answer; compare Q5 rationale | The model answer states that CEA “cannot compare across diseases.” That is too absolute: CEA can compare programmes from different diseases when they share a suitable natural outcome, such as life-years gained. The rationale for Q5 expressly acknowledges this exception. | The model answer contradicts another answer and teaches an incorrect limitation of CEA. | Replace with: “CEA has limited cross-disease comparability unless the interventions use the same meaningful natural outcome.” |
| C-005 | minor | Chapter 5, Q6 and rationale | Q6 asks for the “most important” classification of medicines for marketing and keys supply status. Therapeutic-use classification is also defensible because it determines the clinical audience, competitors and positioning; no objective criterion makes supply status uniquely “most important.” | The MCQ is not single-best-answer despite having one keyed response. | Ask which classification determines whether promotion is directed primarily to prescribers or may, within regulation, address the public. |
| C-006 | minor | Chapter 12, §12.5, “The average ratios…would favour A whatever the decision-maker is willing to pay” | The statement wrongly connects average cost-per-QALY rankings to willingness to pay, then correctly says averages cannot rank options. Depending on the threshold, neither strategy or both strategies may have an average ratio below it; the choice between A and B still requires the incremental result. | It introduces a contradictory decision rule immediately after an otherwise correct ICER calculation. | State that naïvely choosing the lower average ratio would select A, but that this comparison is invalid; use the 30,000 EGP/QALY ICER or incremental net benefit at the chosen threshold. |
