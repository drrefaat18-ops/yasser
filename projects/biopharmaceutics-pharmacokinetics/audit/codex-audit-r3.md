|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | major | Chapter 2, §2.4, “A low Vd, roughly 0.1 to 1 L·kg⁻¹, indicates a drug held in the plasma” | The stated range is physiologically wrong. A Vd near 1 L·kg⁻¹ exceeds total body water and ordinarily indicates substantial tissue distribution, not confinement to plasma. | It teaches students to misclassify distribution from Vd. | Replace the range with approximate reference volumes: plasma about 0.04 L·kg⁻¹, extracellular fluid about 0.2 L·kg⁻¹, and total body water about 0.6 L·kg⁻¹; explain that values above total body water imply tissue binding/distribution. |
| C-002 | minor | Chapter 1, E1.2 worked solution, “tmax … is unchanged” followed by “tmax needs Chapter 7” | The solution both gives tmax confidently and says it cannot be predicted confidently from Chapter 1, even though §1.3 already states that tmax is dose-independent under the model. | The stated answer to the exercise’s confidence question is internally inconsistent. | State that all three predictions follow under the explicitly assumed linear, unchanged-parameter model, while noting that Chapter 7 supplies the derivation. |
| C-003 | major | Chapter 3, §3.5 table “Order of elimination”; Q10 and its rationale | The comparison table says the urinary excretion-rate method works for zero- and first-order elimination, but Q10 keys “neither applies” under zero-order elimination. The rationale silently narrows “method” to the first-order semilog equation. | The chapter gives mutually incompatible teaching and makes two MCQ options defensible depending on which section is followed. | Distinguish the raw observed-rate plot from the first-order semilog rate method. State explicitly that raw rates can describe zero-order excretion, whereas Equation 3.2 cannot estimate a first-order K or half-life from such data. Rewrite Q10 accordingly. |
| C-004 | major | Chapter 6, §6.4, “In most … literature fe means the fraction unbound … and does not use fu at all” | Standard notation is the reverse: `f_e` denotes fraction excreted unchanged and `f_u` denotes fraction unbound. The chapter later uses the nonstandard `fe,unbound`, contradicting this claim. | Reversing two common pharmacokinetic symbols creates errors when students use external sources or filtration equations. | Use `f_e` for fraction excreted unchanged and `f_u` for fraction unbound throughout; replace `fe,unbound × GFR` with `f_u × GFR`. |
| C-005 | major | Chapter 6, §6.6, “Inulin or creatinine clearance supplies the reference, since both are filtered only” | Creatinine is not filtered only; it also undergoes tubular secretion. The glossary itself correctly says creatinine clearance overestimates true GFR for this reason. | It makes the renal-clearance comparison internally contradictory and can produce a biased filtration reference. | Say that inulin is filtered without secretion or reabsorption; describe creatinine clearance as a practical but modestly high estimate of GFR because of secretion. |
| C-006 | major | Chapter 6, §6.6 clearance-ratio table, “Less than 1 — Filtered, then partially reabsorbed” | A renal clearance below GFR does not establish reabsorption. Protein binding alone can give this result, as Example 6.1 later acknowledges. | Students may infer a renal mechanism from a ratio that measures only net handling. | Compare renal clearance with `f_u × GFR`, not unadjusted GFR, and label a low unadjusted ratio as compatible with binding and/or net reabsorption. |
| C-007 | major | Chapter 6, §6.4, “Formation clearances for every metabolite, plus Clr, sum to ClT” | This equality is not generally valid without complete recovery, mutually exclusive primary pathways, molar or stoichiometric correction, and accounting for other unchanged-drug elimination routes and sequential metabolites. | It converts an assay-specific metabolite recovery calculation into an unjustified total-clearance mass balance. | State the restrictive mass-balance conditions explicitly and otherwise describe measured formation clearances as partial apparent clearances that need not sum to total clearance. |
| C-008 | major | Chapter 7, Example 7.1, “80% is absorbed” followed by “F = 0.80” | The example equates fraction absorbed with systemic bioavailability despite §7.1 explicitly distinguishing `f_a` from `F = f_aF_gF_h`. | The resulting calculation is valid only if gut-wall and hepatic availability both equal one, an assumption not stated in the problem. | Change the input to “bioavailability is 0.80,” or provide `f_a = 0.80` together with `F_g = F_h = 1`. |
| C-009 | major | Chapter 8, §8.4 Equation 8.2 and “time taken to reach half of the steady-state level”; glossary “accumulation half-life” | The derivation calculates the fraction of steady-state **interval AUC**, not a universal fraction of peak, trough, or concentration at a fixed post-dose time. Those concentration trajectories depend on dosing interval and phase within the interval. | The text generalizes one exposure-area result into a route-wide time-to-steady-state rule and then says it is independent of dosing frequency. | Name the result “time for interval AUC to reach 50% of its steady-state value.” Derive peak/trough or phase-specific convergence separately, or restrict the general steady-state statements to IV infusion/bolus cases where they apply. |
| C-010 | major | Chapter 8, E8.1–E8.2; Chapter 9, §9.5 procainamide example | The rewritten book retains DeHaan, Regamey and procainamide study data while expressly admitting that no traceable citation was found. This leaves first-edition finding F-031 unresolved for those named studies even though the ledger marks only the Amidon-date portion fixed. | Readers cannot verify whether the attributed studies, populations or numerical data are authentic. | Locate and cite the primary reports. If they cannot be traced, remove the investigator attributions and label the numbers as constructed teaching datasets. |
| C-011 | major | Chapter 9, §9.1 “Only one assumption is needed: the kinetics are linear”; Q1; glossary “Non-compartmental analysis” | NCA can calculate descriptive AUC, AUMC, MRT and dose-specific apparent parameters from nonlinear data. Linearity is required for dose scaling, superposition and dose-invariant interpretation, not for performing NCA. Chapter 10 §10.1 correctly says NCA still runs under nonlinear kinetics, directly contradicting Chapter 9 and its keyed MCQ. | The definition and assessment key are factually wrong and obscure the distinction between computation and cross-dose inference. | State that NCA does not require linearity for dose-specific descriptive analysis. Rewrite Q1 around the absence of a compartmental-model assumption and explain where linearity is additionally required. |
| C-012 | minor | Chapter 10, Q6, “For a drug with non-linear kinetics, the elimination half-life … rises as elimination saturates” | Nonlinear kinetics are not synonymous with saturable elimination; nonlinear absorption, binding or induction can yield different half-life behaviour. The keyed option is correct only for the narrower mechanism discussed later. | The stem makes the keyed answer overbroad and allows objections based on other nonlinear mechanisms. | Change the stem to “For a drug with capacity-limited, saturable elimination as concentration approaches or exceeds Km…”. |
| C-013 | major | Chapter 12, §12.1, “Intact proteins have no carrier … Protein drugs are therefore not absorbed after oral administration” | This categorical conclusion is false. Intact peptide/protein uptake can be small but nonzero through vesicular or receptor-mediated pathways, and absorption-enhancing oral peptide formulations exist. | It replaces the defensible statement “usually degraded and poorly absorbed” with an absolute biological claim. | State that most peptide/protein drugs have extremely low conventional oral bioavailability because of degradation and permeability barriers, while noting limited vesicular/receptor-mediated and formulation-assisted uptake. |
| C-014 | minor | Chapter 12, Equation 12.1 and glossary “permeability coefficient”; `K` defined as lipid/water partition coefficient | `K` is redefined here despite being used throughout the book and glossary for the elimination rate constant. | The same symbol denotes two unrelated quantities in equations that students must combine across chapters. | Use `K_part`, `K_o/w`, or another explicitly distinct symbol for the partition coefficient. |
| C-015 | major | Chapter 12, §12.6, “Solutions. Absorption is rapid and complete” | A solution removes dissolution as a limiting step but does not guarantee rapid or complete systemic absorption. Permeability, degradation, transporters and first-pass loss can still limit rate and extent; the preceding erythromycin example already contradicts the claim. | It confuses being dissolved with being fully bioavailable. | Replace with “Solutions often permit faster absorption because no dissolution step is required; completeness still depends on stability, permeability and presystemic loss.” |
| C-016 | major | Chapter 13, Figure 13.1 caption and following paragraph, “C has the same tmax as A and so the same rate” | Equal tmax does not establish equal absorption rate. tmax depends jointly on absorption, elimination, lag time and curve shape; different absorption profiles can share the same peak time. | This is presented as the central skill for interpreting rate and extent and therefore teaches an invalid inference. | Describe tmax as an observed rate-related endpoint, not a unique measure of Ka. Require the full concentration–time profile and Cmax, or a fitted/deconvolved absorption measure, before claiming equal rates. |
| C-017 | major | Chapter 14, §14.4 acid-stage A1 criterion; Q8 and rationale, “less than 10%” | USP `<711>` states that no individual A1 value may **exceed** 10% dissolved; exactly 10% therefore passes. The book requires strictly less than 10%, and the same boundary error is keyed in Q8. | This is an incorrect pharmacopoeial acceptance criterion and can change a batch decision at the boundary. | Replace “less than 10%” with “not more than 10%” or “≤10%” in the section, question and answer rationale. |
| C-018 | minor | Glossary entries `Ka`, `Ke` and `Vp`, “See [[…]]” | These are wiki-link tokens, not valid links in the Markdown used by the book, so the cross-references do not resolve. | The glossary’s explicit navigation references are broken. | Replace them with valid Markdown anchor links, such as `[absorption rate constant](#absorption-rate-constant)`. |
