## What the source is

Revision notes for three lectures: entrepreneurship (definitions, elements, participants, characteristics, stages,
classification, development programmes, why entrepreneurship matters and how to promote it); global organisations
(the task and general environment, outsourcing, barriers to entry); and national culture (Hofstede, globalisation).
About two-thirds of the text is assessment: 15 MCQs without rationales, 20 true/false items and a 30-item bank with
answers and rationales. Three short foreign cases (Apple, Alibaba, Tesla) close the notes. They are revision bullets,
partly generated by a chatbot, not teaching text.

## What the source gets right

The syllabus of the three lectures is a sound opening for the course. The core elements of entrepreneurship
(innovation, risk-taking, vision, organising), the characteristics list and the task-versus-general-environment
distinction are correct. Most of the 30-item bank's keys are right, but several items are arguable or have two
defensible answers, and the uncertainty-avoidance items state the direction of the scale inconsistently (F-029).

## What must change

**Health and Egypt (D1, the one blocker).** The notes contain no health content and no Egyptian content. The readers
are third-year Applied Health Sciences students, and the user asked for examples of Egyptian health entrepreneurs.
Every concept must be applied to health ventures, with laboratory and imaging perspectives, and every chapter needs a
verified Egyptian example. Ethics and regulation are absent; profit is the only aim in the definitions.

**The venture-creation path (G2, D2).** The notes define the entrepreneur but never teach how to start a venture. The
rework adds need finding, customer discovery, design and the MVP, the business model, costs and break-even, funding,
regulation, intellectual property and launch, with worked health examples whose arithmetic is checked. Chanda and
Gupta (2025) serve as the structural reference, cited, with none of their text reproduced.

**Definitions (G1).** The definitions describe a manufacturer, not an entrepreneur: no opportunity, and services are
excluded. Hofstede is given as five dimensions (six since 2010), and the five-dimension model is built into the
questions. The typologies mix schemes without a source, "Dwadlers" is a misspelling, and the four-stage model is
unsourced but tested as fact. The cases are dated, and one invites a false founder claim (Tesla was founded by
Eberhard and Tarpenning; Musk joined as an investor).

**Assessment (G3).** The first ten MCQs have no key; distractors are absurd negations; the 30-item key is 14 B and
12 C. Several items are arguable or have two defensible answers (the restaurant "startup", the Latin etymology, the
globalisation cause, the Alibaba case), four pairs of items duplicate each other, and the uncertainty-avoidance
items disagree with their own rationale.

**Sources (G4).** There is not one citation.
