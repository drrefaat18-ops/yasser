# Errata Ledger

Every factual error found in the source slides during evaluation, and how this edition handles it. "Fixed" means the chapter now states the corrected fact.

| Erratum | Finding | Chapter | Source line | Error | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E001 | F-001 | ch01 | 34 | Early-onset Alzheimer's disease is defined as onset below 65 years, not below 40; the 1% figure applies to autosomal-dominant familial disease. | fixed |
| E002 | F-005 | ch02 | 264 | Positive symptoms are linked to mesolimbic dopamine excess; the mesocortical pathway is hypoactive in schizophrenia. | fixed |
| E003 | F-006 | ch02 | 275 | Negative symptoms are attributed to serotonin; the accepted model links them to mesocortical dopamine hypofunction (5-HT2A block helps indirectly). | fixed |
| E004 | F-007 | ch02 | 324 | Extrapyramidal effects are misclassified: acute dystonia is reversible; tardive dyskinesia (late, potentially irreversible) and drug-induced parkinsonism are missing. | fixed |
| E005 | F-009 | ch02 | 383 | The text says FGAs treat only negative symptoms; FGAs mainly treat positive symptoms. | fixed |
| E006 | F-010 | ch02 | 420 | Metabolic effects of SGAs are attributed to 5-HT2A block; they are mainly due to H1 and 5-HT2C antagonism. | fixed |
| E007 | F-015 | ch03 | 711 | Levodopa's short half-life is attributed to hepatic decarboxylases; peripheral AADC (gut wall, blood vessels) and COMT are responsible, and on/off fluctuations reflect disease progression plus short half-life. | fixed |
| E008 | F-016 | ch03 | 767 | Dark urine and sweat with levodopa come from oxidation of catecholamine metabolites (melanin-like pigments), not specifically homovanillic acid. | fixed |
| E009 | F-017 | ch03 | 777 | Pyridoxine is said to be contraindicated with levodopa; the interaction applies to levodopa alone and is abolished by carbidopa, which almost all patients take. | fixed |
| E010 | F-019 | ch03 | 818 | Pulmonary fibrosis is called 'very specific to bromocriptine'; fibrotic reactions are an ergot-derivative class effect (also cardiac valvulopathy). | fixed |
| E011 | F-022 | ch04 | 1036 | MAO-B is said to be in brain only and metabolise dopamine only; MAO-B is also in platelets and liver, and MAO-A metabolises serotonin, noradrenaline and tyramine. | fixed |
| E012 | F-023 | ch04 | 1061 | Orthostatic hypotension and sedation are listed as effects of all antidepressants due to raised amines; they are due to alpha1 and H1 block (TCAs, trazodone, mirtazapine) and are rare with SSRIs. | fixed |
| E013 | F-025 | ch04 | 1086 | Yogurt is given as the typical tyramine food; the high-risk foods are aged cheese, cured or fermented meats, soy sauce and tap beer. | fixed |
| E014 | F-027 | ch04 | 1115 | 'Nor-amitriptyline' is not a drug name; the drug is nortriptyline. | fixed |
| E015 | F-028 | ch04 | 1130 | TCA sedation is attributed to atropine-like action; it is due to H1 antagonism. | fixed |
| E016 | F-029 | ch04 | 1183 | Routine PPI prescribing for SSRI GI upset is not standard advice; take with food, start low; PPI is considered only with added bleeding risk (NSAIDs, anticoagulants). | fixed |
| E017 | F-030 | ch04 | 1206 | The mechanisms of mirtazapine and trazodone are wrong or incomplete: mirtazapine is mainly an alpha2 antagonist (also 5-HT2, 5-HT3 and H1 block); trazodone is a 5-HT2A antagonist and weak SERT inhibitor. | fixed |
| E018 | F-032 | ch04 | 1189 | SSRI withdrawal is described as an 'acute attack of depression'; it is a discontinuation syndrome (dizziness, paraesthesia, irritability), worst with paroxetine. | fixed |
| E019 | F-033 | ch05 | 1561 | First-generation drugs are said to act mainly on the inhibitory pathway and second-generation on the excitatory pathway; phenytoin and carbamazepine block Na+ channels and several second-generation drugs enhance GABA. The generalisation is false. | fixed |
| E020 | F-034 | ch05 | 1599 | Enzyme induction, megaloblastic anaemia and teratogenicity are said to be common to all first-generation drugs; valproate is an enzyme inhibitor and ethosuximide is not an inducer. | fixed |
| E021 | F-037 | ch05 | 1669 | Valproate's haematological effect is thrombocytopenia (neutropenia is less typical), and the pregnancy rule is a pregnancy-prevention programme rather than an absolute prohibition. | fixed |
| E022 | F-038 | ch05 | 1745 | 'AMBA receptors' should be AMPA/kainate receptors. | fixed |
| E023 | F-039 | ch05 | 1779 | Gabapentin and pregabalin bind the alpha2delta subunit of voltage-gated Ca2+ channels, not 'P/Q calcium channels'. | fixed |
| E024 | F-041 | ch05 | 1489 | The pathophysiology sentence is garbled and the absence-seizure mechanism ('during the day causing a sleep-like state') is unclear. | fixed |
| E025 | F-042 | ch06 | 1942 | Baclofen is said to increase chloride influx; GABA-B is a G-protein-coupled receptor that opens K+ channels and reduces presynaptic Ca2+ influx. | fixed |
| E026 | F-043 | ch06 | 2078 | 'hydration derivative' should read 'hydantoin derivative'. | fixed |
| E027 | F-044 | ch06 | 2109 | The classification table says depolarising blockers cause hyperpolarisation; they cause persistent depolarisation. | fixed |
| E028 | F-047 | ch06 | 2173 | 'These agents are safe with minimal side effects' ignores histamine release (atracurium, mivacurium), residual paralysis and awareness risk. | fixed |
| E029 | F-048 | ch07 | 2355 | Benzodiazepine half-life classes are wrong: diazepam and clonazepam are long-acting (diazepam 20-80 h plus active nordiazepam); oxazepam is short-intermediate; lorazepam, used later, is missing. | fixed |
| E030 | F-049 | ch07 | 2347 | The mechanism slide contradicts itself: it says BZs increase channel opening frequency, then that they 'do not affect the opening of the ion channel'. | fixed |
| E031 | F-050 | ch07 | 2536 | Benzodiazepines are called 'very safe' with a toxic dose 1000-fold the therapeutic dose; combined with alcohol, opioids or other depressants they cause fatal respiratory depression (FDA boxed warning). | fixed |
| E032 | F-051 | ch07 | 2541 | Flumazenil is an antagonist at the benzodiazepine site, not a GABA receptor antagonist. | fixed |
| E033 | F-052 | ch07 | 2578 | Buspirone acts as a 5-HT1A partial agonist; the 5-HT2A attribution is wrong. | fixed |
| E034 | F-053 | ch07 | 2704 | Barbiturates are anticonvulsants; it is their withdrawal that lowers the seizure threshold. | fixed |
| E035 | F-055 | ch07 | 2796 | Doxepin is a tricyclic antidepressant; its hypnotic effect at low dose is from H1 antagonism, not an SNRI mechanism. | fixed |
| E036 | F-057 | ch08 | 2997 | Malignant hyperthermia is said to occur when anaesthetics are used 'with' succinylcholine; either trigger alone can cause it. | fixed |
| E037 | F-059 | ch08 | 3096 | Ketamine 'does not affect respiration' overstates; it causes minimal respiratory depression and preserves airway reflexes, but increases secretions. | fixed |
| E038 | F-061 | ch09 | 3236 | Amides are labelled 'topical local anesthetics for the eyes'; the eye agents are esters/amides such as tetracaine and proxymetacaine; amides are mainly injected. | fixed |
| E039 | F-072 | ch01 | 71 | Cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine are presented as slowing disease progression; they are symptomatic treatments with modest, temporary benefit and do not modify the disease course (repeated at lines 101-104 and 196-228). | fixed |
| E040 | F-073 | ch04 | 1185 | SSRI sexual dysfunction is said to disappear after two weeks; nausea often settles, but sexual dysfunction commonly persists and must be reviewed. | fixed |
| E041 | F-074 | ch03 | 824 | Amantadine is said to potentiate dopamine reuptake; it increases release, inhibits reuptake and antagonises NMDA receptors (useful for levodopa-induced dyskinesia). | fixed |
| E042 | F-075 | ch05 | 1557 | Loss of consciousness is said to be constant in all generalised seizures; awareness can be preserved in myoclonic seizures. | fixed |
| E043 | F-076 | ch06 | 2164 | Vecuronium is said to form inactive metabolites; its 3-desacetyl metabolite is active and can accumulate. | fixed |
| E044 | F-082 | ch06 | 2167 | Pancuronium is said to be eliminated renally unchanged; it is mainly renal but partly hepatic, with an active 3-hydroxy metabolite. | fixed |
